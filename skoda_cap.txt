: Known Faults — Skoda Citigo

: The notes below come from What Car?, Auto Express, Carbuyer, Motoring Research and DVSA. Each Carbuyer owner comment is one person’s experience, and the Motoring Research view comes from one road test.

: Manual Clutch and Gearbox Wear

:  CHECK · Any miles · Get a quote 

: What Car? says quite a few Citigo and Volkswagen Up owners with manual cars have reported excessive clutch and gearbox wear. Its advice for a buyer is to feel for a clear bite point and to accelerate from a low speed in a high gear to be sure the clutch does not slip. It also says to ensure the clutch has not suffered from too much stop-and-start driving. One Carbuyer owner of a 2012 car writes that the clutch makes a noise when the foot comes off the pedal. The same owner says Skoda call it a known problem but cannot fix it until they know how to.

: Automated Manual Gearbox

:  MONITOR · Any miles · Get a quote 

: What Car? describes the AGR gearbox as a crude automated manual that results in glacially slow gearchanges and jerky progress. Motoring Research tested a Citigo Elegance 1.0 MPI and wrote that its semi-automatic transmission must be one of the most disappointing systems on the market. That is the verdict of one road test. A Carbuyer owner of a 2013 1.0 MPI says the automatic gears stay in second going up hills. Drive any automatic Citigo on a route with a slope before you commit.

: Daytime Running Bulbs

:  MINOR · Any miles · Get a quote 

: A Carbuyer owner of a 2013 Citigo says it seems to eat daylight running bulbs. This is one owner’s remark. Check that the lights come on with the ignition when you view a car.

: Urban Dents and Scratches

:  MINOR · Any miles · Get a quote 

: What Car? tells buyers to look for the kind of dents and scratches that are often inflicted on cars that spend their time in mainly urban environments. Walk round every panel in good light, and ask the seller where the car has been used.

: Citigo e iV Battery

:  CHECK · Any miles · Ask a Skoda dealer 

: For the electric Citigo e iV, What Car? says the battery is covered by a warranty lasting eight years or 100,000 miles. Ask for the car’s service history and warranty paperwork before you buy a used one.

: Safety Recalls

:  CHECK · Any miles · Ask a Skoda dealer 

: DVSA’s recall data lists several Citigo recalls, including these.
<ul>

: On affected vehicles damaged battery cells could have been installed in the high voltage battery which could lead to a thermal overload

: The welding of the towing eye in the vehicle tool kit may not be to specification

: The front panel of the panoramic sunroof may not have been bonded correctly which can lead to rattling noises during a journey

: The door child locks may disengage without driver knowledge

: Some vehicles may be fitted with airbag control units with incorrect parameters programmed
</ul>

: A car can have had its recall work done or still be waiting for it. Enter the registration in our recall check to see where a particular car stands.

: Buying a used Skoda Citigo

: What Car? reports generally positive opinions from owners about the reliability and dealer service of the Skoda Citigo, and Auto Express writes that all three models in its Citigo review have so far proved reliable, with few common problems to report.

: Start with the gearbox. On a manual, test the clutch for a clear bite point and for slip, and think about how much town driving the car has done. On an automatic, drive it and see whether the changes suit you.
